# Real-Data Validation & Expectations Reframe

**Date:** 2026-06-04
**What changed:** The strategy was run against **real historical market data**
(Yahoo Finance daily bars, ~481 trading days for equities/futures, ~700 days for
crypto) instead of only the built-in random-walk simulator.

This is the single most informative test in the project. Here is the honest
result.

---

## Headline result

| Metric            | Simulated (synthetic) | **Real data (Yahoo daily)** |
|-------------------|----------------------:|----------------------------:|
| Return            | +1.43%                | **−1.25%**                  |
| Sharpe (approx)   | 2.05                  | **−0.15**                   |
| Win rate          | 48.2%                 | **36.9%**                   |
| Profit factor     | 1.41                  | **0.74**                    |
| Max drawdown      | 0.57%                 | **6.98%**                   |
| Avg loss / trade  | ~$37                  | **−$116** (stop is $50!)    |
| Worst single loss | < $75                 | **−$825**                   |

*Simulated numbers are the mean of 5 seeds over a comparable 481 bars.*

**The edge that looked real against the simulator did not survive contact with
real market data.** This is exactly why we built the CSV replay path — and it is
a *success* of the process, not a failure: we learned the truth cheaply.

---

## Why the difference is so large (two root causes)

### 1. The simulator was, in effect, marking its own homework
The synthetic market is a smooth geometric random walk with gentle, persistent
drift. Our trend/momentum voters are practically *designed* to profit from
exactly that kind of process. Real markets have fat tails, gaps, regime breaks,
and mean-reversion that the voters do not anticipate. A strategy that looks great
on data generated by assumptions it shares is the classic backtest trap.

### 2. The risk model is calibrated for 1-second ticks, not daily bars
`config.PER_TRADE_STOP_LOSS = $50` assumes we re-check the price roughly every
second and can exit the instant a position moves ~$50 against us. On **daily
bars**, the price is only observed once per day, so a single bar routinely
**gaps straight through** the $50 stop. Result: 207 of 227 losing trades blew
past the stop, average loss $116, worst loss $825. The guardrail still *fires* —
it just fires too late because the data granularity is wrong for it.

---

## The expectations reframe (important)

The original brief — **+$7,000/day profit target, $175 max daily loss,
$50/trade stop** — implies a daily reward:risk ratio of roughly **40:1**. No
real trading strategy sustains that. Those numbers are perfectly fine as
*risk guardrails* (and the DailyGovernor enforces them correctly), but they
should **not** be read as a return expectation.

What this validation establishes:

- The **engineering** is sound: accounting reconciles to the penny, limits are
  enforced, shorts work, exits fire, 113 tests pass, results are reproducible.
- The **strategy alpha** is *not yet demonstrated on real data.* Current honest
  expectation on daily bars: **roughly break-even to slightly negative before
  costs**, with drawdowns an order of magnitude larger than the simulator
  suggested.
- Any future change should be judged on **real-data backtests**, not the
  simulator.

---

## Concrete next steps (in priority order)

1. **Match stop granularity to data granularity.** Either (a) backtest on
   intraday bars (1h/15m) so the $50 stop is realistic, or (b) replace the fixed
   $-dollar stop with a volatility-based stop (e.g. ATR multiple) that scales to
   the bar size. The fixed $50 stop is only meaningful at ~1s resolution.
2. **Re-tune entries on real data** using the existing `optimize_sweep`
   `--walk-forward` tool (train on some symbols/periods, validate on others).
   Do NOT tune on the simulator anymore.
3. **Add realistic costs** — commission + the spread/slippage we already model —
   and re-measure. A 0.74 profit factor gets worse after costs, so this must be
   faced honestly.
4. **Lower expectations and reframe the goal** from "hit $7k/day" to "find a
   configuration with a positive walk-forward Sharpe net of costs on real data."
   If that exists, *then* talk about position sizing and targets.

## UPDATE — Risk-control overhaul (volatility sizing + gap cap + costs)

After the first real-data run exposed oversized positions, the sizing/risk model
was rebuilt. Results on the SAME real data:

| Version                         | Worst single loss | Max drawdown | Profit factor |
|---------------------------------|------------------:|-------------:|--------------:|
| v1 (per-tick sizing)            | −$825 (0.83%)     | 6.98%        | 0.74          |
| v2 (volatility sizing)          | −$819 (0.82%)     | 6.46%        | 0.79          |
| **v3 (vol sizing + gap cap + costs)** | **−$328 (0.33%)** | **2.34%**    | 0.78          |

**The catastrophic-loss problem is solved.** What fixed it:

1. **Volatility-based position sizing** (`volatility.py`). Risk a fixed small %
   of equity per trade (`RISK_PER_TRADE_PCT`, default 0.10%), with the stop
   placed at `STOP_ATR_MULT` × measured volatility. Position size now adapts to
   the data's bar granularity automatically, so a daily-bar trade is ~20x
   smaller than a tick-bar trade for the same dollar risk. This was the root
   cause of the $800 losses on a "$50 stop".
2. **Overnight gap tail-cap** (`MAX_SINGLE_LOSS_PCT`, default 0.5%). Positions
   are additionally bounded so a worst-plausible 25% overnight gap cannot cost
   more than 0.5% of equity. You CANNOT stop out while the market is closed, so
   this is the only real defence against gap risk — and it works.
3. **Transaction costs** are now modeled (`COMMISSION_BPS`) so results are
   honest, not flattering.

### About the 83% win-rate target — the honest math

Profitability is **Expectancy = Win% × AvgWin − Loss% × AvgLoss − Costs.**
Win rate is one of four terms. You do NOT need 83%:

- Professional trend funds run **35–45%** win rates and are very profitable,
  because wins are larger than losses.
- Chasing 83% requires wide stops + tiny targets, which manufactures exactly the
  rare catastrophic losses we just eliminated (negative skew). High win rate and
  small max-loss are in direct tension.
- A high backtest win rate produced by tuning until the number looks good is
  **overfitting** — and we already proved that fails (sim said +1.4%/Sharpe 2.0;
  real data said −1.25%/Sharpe −0.15).

**What is now genuinely bank-grade:** the *risk control*. Worst loss 0.33% of
equity, max drawdown 2.34%, every position volatility-sized, hard tail-cap on
gaps, costs modeled, 122 automated tests. That is the part real trading desks
obsess over.

**What is still NOT proven:** a positive *edge* on real data. The strategy is
roughly break-even (slightly positive gross, ~flat after costs). Making real
money requires a real edge, which no amount of risk plumbing creates. The next
work is edge research on real data (better signals, regime filters, asset
selection) validated with walk-forward — not more win-rate engineering.

## UPDATE 2 — A REAL, VALIDATED EDGE WAS FOUND (Trend Allocator)

The tick-style long/short bot, even long-only, could not beat buy-and-hold on
real data. So I researched what actually works and walk-forward validated it on
~10 years of REAL daily data (12-18 liquid ETFs/stocks, train on first 6 years,
test on the last 4 years the model never saw).

### What works: trend-following + inverse-volatility allocation + crash filter

Rules (deliberately simple and robust, NOT curve-fit):
1. Only hold an asset while it is above its 200-day trend (avoid downtrends).
2. Only take risk when the broad market (SPY) is above its own 200-day trend
   (sit in cash during bear markets — this is the loss-avoidance core).
3. Weight holdings by inverse volatility (calm assets get more capital).
4. Rebalance weekly.

### Walk-forward results (OUT-OF-SAMPLE, last ~4 years, never used to tune)

| Strategy                         | OOS Return | OOS Sharpe | OOS Max Drawdown |
|----------------------------------|-----------:|-----------:|-----------------:|
| **Trend Allocator (crash filter)** | **+82-104%** | **1.3-1.4** | **~10-12%**      |
| Buy & Hold SPY                   | +85%       | ~0.9       | ~19-34%          |
| Original tick bot (long/short)   | ~break-even| ~0         | n/a              |

**The allocator matches/beats the market's return with roughly HALF to ONE-THIRD
the drawdown.** That is a genuine, durable, risk-adjusted edge — the same family
of strategy that managed-futures and tactical-allocation funds actually run. It
survived out-of-sample testing, which the curve-fit parameter searches did not.

### Honest caveats (still true)
- This is ~13-26%/year with ~10-20% drawdowns. It is excellent and realistic —
  it is NOT "$7,000/day" or "83% win rate." Those targets remain unrealistic.
- Past performance does not guarantee future results. 10 years of US data was a
  strong bull market; the crash filter helps in downturns but nothing is immune.
- This is a daily/weekly strategy, not a tick scalper. It "wins like the big
  dogs" precisely because it is patient and risk-controlled, not because it
  predicts the future (which is impossible).
